GCC 13 CoreMark operand coverage, Windows x64 and macOS ARM64

The 776-entry production plan keeps all 704 original entries in order and
appends the 8 most frequent missing fused shapes and 64 single-op shapes
from the static GCC 13.3 i686 ELF documented in ../coremark-static.md.
These are encoding facts only (ModRM, prefix, address descriptor), not guest
addresses, displacements, immediate values, or data. All targets share the
same plan; the default GPR pinning policy is unchanged.

Coverage was estimated by weighting decoded shapes in a WRITE_BACK block
dump by their block-entry execution counts. This is not a retired guest
instruction counter. The original plan covered 48.84% of the old guest
and 90.11% of the Alpine GCC 15.2 guest. The expanded plan covers 92.19%
and 92.33% respectively. Coverage of MOVSX/MOVZX/IMUL shapes rises from
21.37% to 88.57% on the GCC 13 guest (94.54% unchanged on GCC 15).
In the previous x64 sample, generic MOVSX loaded pinned values via stack
temporaries and tested the register index at runtime before address/operand
selection. The new OpMovsx_Word_32_Edx handler for shape 34494742547 has
no stack spill or dynamic pin checks on its TLB hit path; it uses fixed
state offsets and a word load followed by cwde. The TLB/redo checks remain.

Windows x64 Release (same managed CLI and fusion source), three paired
45,000-iteration runs for GCC 13, two 60,000-iteration runs for GCC 15,
all CRC validated:

Guest   Plan       OFF median    WRITE_BACK median    Median paired pin delta
GCC 13  original     2738.89             2488.39                     -8.84%
GCC 13  expanded     3401.87             3348.46                     -1.54%
GCC 15  original     4149.68             4328.54                     +4.32%
GCC 15  expanded     4143.73             4309.02                     +3.99%

The increased coverage explains most of the GCC 13 pin penalty but does
not eliminate it. The plan improves the unpinned GCC 13 build too.

On M5 Max/ARM64 WRITE_BACK the initial GCC 15 score declined 1.30% (median
of three paired runs). A separate three-pair libproc check reproduced
+0.89% cycles and -0.05% retired instructions, but not a consistent score
drop (paired throughput median +0.16%). On a separate Time Profiler pair,
the share at typical next-handler pointer loads went from 26.32% to 27.73%;
load/store PCs went from 55.34% to 57.69%. Existing TEST/Jcc and MOV fusion
handlers gained sample share, even where their instruction addresses
stayed the same. The .text section grew from 1,745,572 to 1,754,640 bytes.
The signal is extra cycles around existing threaded dispatch/loads, not
additional retired work. PC samples cannot establish cache-miss, branch
prediction, or alignment causality. The standard Instruments template
does not supply the needed per-event hardware counts without configuration.
